# Investor Deck slide types

Every slide has one narrative job, one dominant visual hierarchy, and one focus
color. Hero means a thesis-led field, Split means a deliberate two-region
composition, and Frame means information organized by a visible system.

| Type | Best canvas | Logic | Maximum information | Visual placement | Prohibited |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cover | Black | Hero | title, subline, date | native dark illustration or open field; Level 1 logo | agenda, metrics, dense subtitle |
| Statement | Light or Black | Hero | one claim, one proof | 52–64 pt statement; optional editorial word | paragraph, multi-card grid |
| Problem Cards | Light | Frame | three problems | three flat aligned regions; optional small icons | shadowed dashboard cards, four-plus claims |
| System Map | Light or Black | Frame | five major nodes, seven links | map in 7–9 columns; thesis beside/above | spaghetti connectors, unlabeled decoration |
| Product Screenshot Split | Light | Split | one workflow, three callouts | legible real UI in 7 columns | fake UI, illegible full-app screenshot |
| Process | Light | Frame | three to five stages | horizontal or stepped sequence | circular arrows for a non-recurring process |
| Architecture Layers | Light | Frame | four to six layers | stacked flat bands with direct labels | pseudo-3D cloud architecture, tiny service names |
| Metric Grid | Light | Frame | four to six metrics | direct numbers with date/unit | unsourced vanity metrics, tiny chart multiples |
| Comparison Matrix | Light | Frame | five criteria, five entities | flat matrix; highlight only decisive cells | invented competitor claims, checkmark wall |
| Community Network | Black or Light | Hero/Frame | one network thesis, three proof points | illustration in 7–8 columns, copy in open field | duplicate global-network art in one deck |
| Flywheel | Light | Frame | five or six recurring stages | isometric or flat loop with one active stage | using a loop for a linear funnel |
| Revenue Bridge | Light | Frame | current, drivers, target | waterfall/bridge with dated assumptions | unsupported forecast presented as actual |
| Roadmap | Light | Frame | three horizons, six milestones | horizontal horizons and dependency labels | calendar wallpaper, exact dates without owners |
| Team | Light | Frame | six leaders maximum | consistent portraits or role-led rows | decorative illustration, biographies in 10 pt |
| Use of Funds | Light | Frame | four uses, milestones, runway | proportional bars plus outcome column | unexplained pie, financing claim without period |
| Close | Black | Hero | one closing thesis, contact | Level 1 Inverse or Stacked logo, large open field | recap grid, new evidence, CTA pill |

## Type rules

### Cover

- Title 52–64 pt; supporting line 16–20 pt.
- Use Level 1 brand signature at 48–92 px high.
- Focus is either the yellow brand detail or one yellow illustration path.
- Keep at least half the canvas visually quiet.

### Statement

- The claim must be understandable without speaker narration.
- If a number is the statement, include its unit, period, and source on-slide.
- One short Tinos Italic Latin word may create editorial contrast.

### Problem Cards

- Each region contains one problem headline and one evidence line.
- Use rules/alignment instead of container shadows.
- Keep body text at 16 pt or larger.

### System Map

- Label nodes directly and establish one obvious reading path.
- Black System Frame is appropriate for a chapter-level strategic model.
- Dense technical architecture belongs on a Light Architecture Layers page.

### Product Screenshot Split

- Show a real, approved product state at a legible crop.
- Callouts describe user value or verified behavior, not speculative features.
- Do not use scientific illustration as a substitute for product evidence.

### Process and Architecture Layers

- Use Process for temporal movement and Architecture Layers for dependency or
  abstraction.
- Give every stage/layer an action-oriented label.
- One yellow stage may indicate current position; orange only marks a real risk.

### Metric Grid and Revenue Bridge

- Every value shows unit, date/period, scope, and status: actual, forecast, or
  target.
- Keep source text at 10–12 pt and full methods in speaker notes.
- Prefer direct labels and simple bars/lines to legends and ornamental charts.

### Comparison Matrix

- Compare only public, date-stamped facts on consistently defined criteria.
- Use “not publicly verified” rather than converting missing evidence to “no.”
- Do not assign a synthetic total score unless the weighting method is shown.

### Community Network and Flywheel

- Illustration is allowed because relationships are the evidence structure.
- Pair illustration with at most three quantified or sourced proof points.
- A flywheel must identify the output that causally feeds the next input.

### Roadmap

- Use Now / Next / Later or three dated horizons.
- Separate committed milestones from directional options.
- Include owner/category and evidence of dependency when relevant.

### Team

- Lead with role-market fit and execution evidence.
- Keep names, portraits, and biographies out of reusable template examples.
- Use the actual deck's approved data only during production.

### Use of Funds

- Show allocation, intended milestone, and time horizon together.
- Percentages must total 100% when presented as a complete allocation.
- Do not infer runway from allocation alone.

### Close

- End with one investable thesis, not a second executive summary.
- Use Inverse on black; Stacked is allowed when centered.
- Contact information is live text and remains within the safe area.
